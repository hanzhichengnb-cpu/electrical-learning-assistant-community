# Teach from the supplied source

## Before writing

Visually inventory each requested physical PDF page. Identify its role, concepts, symbols, conditions, figures, equations, worked steps, conclusions and ambiguity. Plan points around those objects and their connections. Preserve physical page numbering. Extracted text is a locator, not proof of visual fidelity.

For a new concept, ask what a learner needs to understand before using it. Rebuild that prerequisite locally where necessary; another page having mentioned it is not enough. For a long lecture, make a small sparse/formula/diagram pilot where present and test it before continuing. Keep working through the full requested range.

## Write to establish understanding

- Start a technical page with the problem it addresses and its connection to nearby content.
- Attach symbols to physical/diagram objects and their roles: input, output, known, unknown, parameter or intermediate variable. A glossary of names and units is insufficient.
- Show the relation or law used at each important step, the substitution/rearrangement, the conclusion and why it follows. Fill meaningful gaps without inventing source claims.
- Explain conditions where they become necessary. Separate explicitly stated source conditions from conditions required by the derivation and assumptions used only in an added example.
- Read diagrams as connected systems: topology, signal direction, axes, reference directions, boundaries and what changes when a parameter changes.
- Integrate source-grounded technical English at first use. Adapt language to the learner; do not impose an English vocabulary quiz.
- Distinguish original statements, explanatory derivations, supplementary examples and uncertain readings. Ask only when an unreadable source detail materially blocks correctness.
- Close technical pages with justified things to remember, relationships to understand and actions the learner should be able to perform. Do not invent exam predictions or declare mastery.

Use as much necessary explanation as the page warrants. Do not shrink formulas or remove foundations to satisfy an arbitrary length or print-page quota. Avoid padding, repetitive summaries and invented prerequisites.

## Source locators

The region identifies the meaningful source object and its click area. A separate outline may use safe nearby whitespace. Choose the outline edges and badge position after examining actual text, equations, wires, figures and handwriting. Partial edges are allowed; an empty edge list displays only the numbered badge. A small badge with no rectangle is preferable to a rectangle cutting through source content.

Check the actual rendered ordinary and selected marks. Keep different points' objects distinct. Do not make a vague page-wide region simply to include unrelated material.

## Second pass

After drafting, reopen the original and read the exact current lesson:

1. Coverage: every important requested source point and condition is explained; equations, figures and examples correspond to the source.
2. Teaching: concepts are established before inference; symbols have meanings and roles; nontrivial steps and conditions have reasons; additions help rather than create more unexplained load.
3. Geometry: every point's source region, visible edges, badge and same-number lesson refer to the correct object without obscuring source ink.

Record concrete source objects and exact current-body passages. Log and repair real gaps. Do not call the initial drafting pass an independent review; a separate second pass by the same agent is acceptable and must be described accurately. A passing script, long output or strong model name does not establish teaching quality.
