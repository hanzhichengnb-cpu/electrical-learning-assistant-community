# Courseware-grounded written self-tests

Use for post-reading review checklists, writable test PDFs and returned answer sheets in this workspace. The purpose is to reveal whether the learner can independently identify, write, derive and apply the course content. Follow the requested source range; establish it from context when clear.

## Organize connected knowledge

Inspect the original source, including figures and appended technical pages, before selecting tasks. Build an internal source-page coverage map of technical points, their relationships and the specific tasks that assess them. Explain omissions of nontechnical or genuinely repeated material.

The primary organizing unit is a coherent group of related knowledge anchored in a shared source object or problem: a response curve, circuit, block diagram, model, derivation chain or comparison of systems. Let the source determine the relationships and group boundaries; groups may span adjacent pages. Do not force unrelated material into one large graphic or impose one group per slide.

Within each group, connect the relevant quantities, concepts, conditions, formulas and uses. Make those connections part of what the learner does: identify positions on the same curve, relate its measures to formulas, or carry local component relations through a model to an input-output result. A topic heading above disconnected questions, or an image beside unrelated prose, does not establish this structure.

Retain detailed checks of basic knowledge inside the groups. Every foundational formula or local step selected for assessment needs a visible, independently gradable response action. Its presence somewhere in a large worked problem is not evidence that it has been checked. Shared context should connect the subparts while making each possible gap identifiable. Short standalone checks remain appropriate where no useful source-grounded connection exists.

Choose quantity from source coverage, not a fixed question quota. A request for a sample does not by itself authorize reducing detailed coverage to a few representative examples. If only a bounded format demonstration is requested, identify that boundary explicitly.

## Choose the response action from the content

Use the simplest action that exposes the intended ability. Mix formats when useful; do not apply a fixed template or demand novelty.

- For diagrams, curves and spatial relationships, use label removal, identification, annotation of points/intervals/directions, or completion of missing parts. Preserve a faithful source figure or a verified redraw. Hide target labels and adjacent answer explanations while retaining the curve, topology, scale, arrows and conditions needed to solve the task. Do not invent numerical readings from an uncalibrated plot.
- For formulas and derivations, ask for a basic relation, a missing term or step, a derivation from a specified starting point, or a concrete application. Distinguish recall of a relation from choosing and using it. Supply necessary assumptions and quantities without printing the intended result on the answer sheet.
- For circuits and block diagrams, connect signal/component identification, local laws, model construction and the resulting input-output relationship where the source supports them. Use diagrams as the object of the task, including drawing/completion tasks when suitable.
- For system properties, use specified systems, input transformations, bounds or counterexamples. Comparing properties of the same examples can reveal their independence and relationships. Do not substitute general concept paraphrasing or English recitation for these actions.

For example, a transient-response curve can support a connected group covering metric locations and symbols, measurement endpoints, the sustained tolerance-band condition, and peak-excess normalization. Follow the source's measurement convention. This is one useful group structure, not a compulsory graph format for every subject.

## Prepare a writable test and separate key

Keep common figures and conditions close to their subparts. When a group continues across pages, repeat the necessary context or give an exact question/page reference. Allocate space by work required: compact areas for short relations, continuous space for derivations, and space for drawing or annotating. Render formal mathematical notation and inspect the final PDF pages using the available PDF skill.

Keep student givens and reference results separate in the authoring data and the rendered document. Check for answer leakage from formulas, figures and surrounding source text. Preserve prior versions when making a requested revision.

Prepare a separate reference key with source pages, intended action, necessary steps and acceptable equivalent answers. It may remain internal when only a student sample is requested. Identify task-specific conditions and source ambiguities; do not reproduce a known source error as an unquestionable grading rule. Score only what was asked, and do not require mechanical repetition of givens.

## Review coverage and structure separately

Before delivery, assess both:

1. **Coverage:** Do all intended basic and important source points have actual response actions, with complete givens and individually identifiable results? Separate directly checked points from material merely involved in a combined task.
2. **Connected structure:** Does each group let the learner reconstruct meaningful relationships around its shared object or problem? Does the sequence connect identification, local relations and use where relevant? More isolated questions or more images alone do not pass this check.

Also verify source fidelity, mathematical correctness, answer isolation, symbol legibility, usable writing space and group continuation references. Record the source-to-group-to-subtask mapping and review evidence in Staging. An accepted visual example establishes the approach only within the user's stated scope; do not infer that every existing worksheet has been updated or that a new system release is approved.

## Grade the returned work

Report results at two levels: the specific subpart/step and the connections within its knowledge group. Distinguish an unidentified symbol, an incorrect local law, and failure to connect correct relations into a model. Base conclusions only on the submitted work; a blank is not a demonstrated misconception and an unreadable mark needs clarification.

Accept equivalent methods and notation. Locate the first substantive error while retaining credit for independently correct later work; flag flawed or ambiguous prompts rather than assigning their defects to the learner. Target follow-up practice at the observed basic gap or missing connection, without automatically declaring the entire topic unmastered.

Test generation, annotations and grading feedback remain derived learning material. They do not publish Canonical knowledge or establish mastery without evidence. Follow the existing save/review boundary only if the user separately asks to retain formal knowledge.
